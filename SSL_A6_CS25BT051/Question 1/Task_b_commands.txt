QUESTION 1 — PART B

(a) Why does the second surface become flatter away from the origin?

The second surface is

z = sin(x)cos(y)e^(-(x^2+y^2)/20).

The factor e^(-(x^2+y^2)/20) decreases as x and y move farther
from the origin.

Therefore, it reduces the amplitude of sin(x)cos(y) away from
the origin. As a result, the values of z become closer to zero
and the surface becomes flatter.


(b) Where do you observe the largest positive and negative values?

The largest positive and negative values are observed near the
origin.

This is because the exponential factor has its maximum value of
1 at the origin and decreases as the distance from the origin
increases.

The sine and cosine terms produce the positive and negative
oscillations, while the exponential factor reduces their
magnitude away from the origin.


(c) How does changing the sampling resolution affect the plot?

Increasing the sampling resolution means that more points are
used to calculate and display the surface.

A higher sampling resolution produces a smoother and more
accurate surface and represents peaks and valleys better.

A lower sampling resolution can make the surface look rough
or less accurate and may miss some small features.

However, increasing the sampling resolution also increases
the time required to generate the plot.
